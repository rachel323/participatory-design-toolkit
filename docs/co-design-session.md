# 📖 Using the Toolkit in a Co-design Session

This document describes the intended use of the toolkit as a research instrument: preparing a co-design session, using the toolkit to prototype, reflecting together with the participants, and analysing the data the toolkit produces. It complements the installation manual and quick guide, which cover the technical working of the soft- and hardware.
The toolkit allows researchers to rapidly prototype technological interventions in social interaction. Participants do not design with pen and paper: they experience and shape interventions *live*, in interaction with each other.

---


## The toolkit as a research instrument

The toolkit uses a **Wizard-of-Oz (WoZ) setup**. One participant takes the **wizard role**: they watch the interaction between the other participants and, based on their intuition of what is needed, can change the **light, sound and vibration** in the room where the interaction takes place.

This gives the researcher three lenses on the data:

1. **The interaction itself**: how the participants interact under the prompt.
2. **The effect of sensory interventions**: how light, sound and vibration change the interaction.
3. **The wizard's perspective**: what the wizard notices, why they feel the need to intervene, and why they intervene in the way they do.

Because the wizard role rotates among participants, everyone experiences both sides of the intervention, and the researcher learns not only about the interaction but also about how different (neurodiverse) participants read and support each other.

## Preparing a session

Before participants arrive:

- **Set up the toolkit** and verify all output channels (light, sound, vibration) and the dashboard are working (see the installation manual).
- **Select interaction prompts** for the session (see the prompt library below). Mix warm-up, exploration and design prompts, and consider which wizard role each prompt suggests.
- **Prepare consent**: plan the briefing, consent procedure and any demographic data you want to collect.
- **Ensure data collection is in place**: the toolkit's cameras and recorders for the interactions, and the dashboard logging of wizard actions. Check that all recording devices are running before the session starts.
- **Safeguard participants' safety and comfort**: the prompts involve physical movement and non-verbal expression; make sure participants know they can stop or withdraw at any time.

## Running a co-design session

The structure below is an example session; adapt timings and prompts to your study.

1. **Briefing & consent** — explain the session structure and the goal. Let participants give consent and provide demographic data.
2. **Warming-up** — a short series of movement prompts (no wizard role yet) to loosen up, synchronise and become aware of one's own body and the other's.
3. **Toolkit exploration phases** — the core of the session (see below).
4. **Group reflection** — close the session by reflecting together (see below).

### Exploration phases

Each phase follows the same rhythm:

- Give the **prompt** to the participants (e.g. *"Take turns leading: one of you moves, the other — and the wizard — respond."*).
- **An x amount of minutes of interaction** with the prompt. The wizard watches and intervenes with light, sound or vibration as they see fit. The wizard can also signal when to switch roles within the interaction, in a way they find fitting at that moment.
- **An x amount minutes of individual reflection** on the interaction and the wizard role, through a questionnaire.
- **Switch the wizard role** to another participant before the next phase.

### Individual and group reflection

Reflection happens on two levels, deliberately separated:

- **Individual reflection directly after each phase** keeps participants focused on their own feelings and self-perception, rather than judging each other's behaviour.
- **Group reflection at the end of the session** lets participants check each other's intentions and the presumptions they had about one another.

Suggested questions for the group reflection:

*On the social interactions:*

- Was there anything surprising about interacting in this way?
- How did the wizard influence the interaction?
- What did you learn about (neurodiverse) interaction?

*On the designs they came up with:*

- What do you think of the design ideas you generated? Could they be useful? Could they be realised?
- What about the toolkit was most useful in generating ideas?

## Analysing the data

After the session, the researcher can draw on four data sources:

| Data source | Collected via | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Video & audio of the interactions | Toolkit cameras and recorders | How the interaction unfolded under each prompt |
| Wizard action log | Toolkit dashboard | When, how and with which modality the wizard intervened |
| Individual reflections | Questionnaires after each phase | Participants' self-perception and experience of the wizard role |
| Group reflection | Session notes or recording | Shared insights, design ideas, lessons learned |

By combining the wizard action log with the video, the researcher can link each intervention to the moment that prompted it — the felt need to intervene — and to its effect on the interaction. Together with the reflections, this leads to lessons learned about the interaction and to design implications for technological interventions.

## Prompt library

Prompts are grouped by genre. The wizard role describes the role suggested for the wizard in that prompt; roles marked *N.A.* are used before the wizard role is assigned, and roles marked *—* can be assigned freely by the facilitator.

### Warming up

*Synchronising, improvising, self-perception / other perception.*

| Prompt | Genre | Wizard role |
|---|---|---|
| Breath synchronization | Warm-up: synchronising | N.A. (warm-up before wizard assigned) |
| Play "yes, and…" | Warm-up: improvisation | N.A. |
| Stretch all your body parts one by one and focus on the feeling | Warm-up: self-perception | N.A. |
| Mirror each other, move slowly/quickly/big/small | Warm-up: synchronising | Audience/Support |

### Other perception

*Explore / relate / collaborate.*

| Prompt | Genre | Wizard role |
|---|---|---|
| Explore the sensory experience together | Explore | Audience/Support |
| Take turns leading: one of you moves, the other (and the wizard) respond. | Relate | Audience/Support |
| Explain to the wizard how you feel, without using words. | Collaborate | Addressee |
| Pretend you feel what the other feels | Explore | Audience/Support |
| Find a shared rhythm | Relate | Collaborator |
| Have a conversation without words | Relate | — |
